# Behavioral Profile

## Overview
Rodrigo is a direct, data-driven product manager who thrives on autonomy and impact. He communicates clearly, pushes back on assumptions with evidence, and values cross-functional collaboration without the overhead of consensus-seeking. His strongest work emerges when given a hard problem, the data to understand it, and the space to execute.

## Core Drives

| Drive | Level | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Results | High | Judges everything by outcomes, not activity. "We shipped it" is praise; "we had meetings about it" is not. |
| Autonomy | High | Prefers to own the problem end-to-end. Micromanagement or shared ownership without clear boundaries causes friction. |
| Evidence | High | Makes and challenges decisions with data. Dislikes decisions driven by hierarchy or "that's how we've always done it." |
| Collaboration | Medium | Works well cross-functionally but values execution speed over alignment rituals. Prefers async async communication to sync-heavy processes. |
| Structure | Medium | Comfortable with process when it prevents chaos, but will bypass process that slows delivery. |

## Communication Style
- **Direct and concise** — says what he means, doesn't hedge. Emails and documents are shorter than average, denser in signal.
- **Evidence-first** — even informal communication references data or prior results. "I think X" is rare; "Here's what the data shows" is the default.
- **Comfortable with disagreement** — invites challenge to assumptions, doesn't take pushback personally. Expects the same in return.
- **Written > spoken for depth** — prefers async docs for complex topics. Can riff verbally but does his best thinking in writing.
- **Humorous but dry** — uses wit to defuse tension, not to avoid hard topics.

## Strengths (in application context)

| Strength | How to demonstrate in cover letter / Q&A |
|---|---|
| End-to-end ownership | Describe outcomes delivered from problem definition to shipped result: "Identified a 40% drop in checkout completion, ran 3 experiments, and shipped a fix that recovered 35% of the gap within 2 weeks." |
| Data-driven decision-making | Reference specific metrics (not just "improved X" but "improved X by Y% as measured by Z"). |
| Cross-functional execution | Explain how you aligned engineering, design, and stakeholders without needing consensus gatekeeping. Mention specific team sizes and disciplines. |
| User empathy grounded in data | Show how quantitative signals (analytics) and qualitative signals (user research) fed into each other. |
| Adaptability across domains | Highlight diverse product areas (e-commerce checkout, AI platforms, B2B self-serve, PLG growth) as evidence of learning transfer, not lack of focus. |

## Growth Areas (frame positively)

| Area | How to acknowledge without apologizing |
|---|---|
| Impatience with slow cycles | "I push for speed because I've seen how fast execution compounds. I set aggressive timelines and back them with evidence of what's achievable." |
| Preference for written over verbal | "I do my clearest thinking in writing. For complex decisions, I'll share a doc first — it saves everyone time and produces better outcomes than open discussion." |
| Selective delegation | "I pick up work that others deprioritize. I've learned to invest in handoffs when the work outgrows what one person can carry." |

## Thrives In
- High-autonomy environments with outcome-based expectations
- Flat-ish orgs where decisions follow data, not title
- Companies that value speed of learning over being wrong in public
- Teams where cross-functional partners are empowered to push back
- Products with clear user problems and measurable success criteria

## Maps to Job Posting Language

**When a JD mentions these → strong behavioral fit:**
- "End-to-end ownership" or "own the product from ideation to launch"
- "Data-informed decision-making" or "metrics-driven"
- "Cross-functional collaboration without heavy process"
- "Comfortable with ambiguity" or "thrives in unstructured environments"
- "Self-starter" or "bias for action"
- "Move fast" or "iterate quickly"

**When a JD mentions these → potential friction (not deal-breaker):**
- "Highly matrixed organization" — may mean slow consensus decision-making
- "Executive stakeholder management" — could mean many alignment meetings
- "Detailed process documentation" — may value compliance over speed
- "Agile ceremonies" with daily standups, retros, grooming — heavy sync overhead
